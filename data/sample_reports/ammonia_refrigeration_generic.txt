PROCESS SAFETY INCIDENT REPORT — Ammonia Refrigeration System Release
Date: August 2019
Location: Food Processing Facility (anonymised)
Source: Generic incident report based on multiple AIChE EPSC case studies

PROCESS DESCRIPTION

The facility operates a large industrial ammonia refrigeration system used for
cold storage of food products. The system contains approximately 15,000 kg of
anhydrous ammonia refrigerant operating in a closed-loop cycle.

The refrigeration system consists of:
- Ammonia compressors (3 × reciprocating compressors, 150 kW each)
- High-pressure receiver vessel (10,000 litre, design pressure 25 barg)
- Low-pressure receiver / surge drum (15,000 litre, design pressure 10 barg)
- Shell and tube evaporators in cold storage rooms
- Shell and tube condenser (water-cooled)
- Expansion valves and associated pipework
- Refrigeration pipework throughout the facility (DN25 to DN150)

Operating conditions:
- High-side pressure: 14–16 barg (condensing)
- Low-side pressure: 1.5–3.5 barg (evaporating)
- Ammonia inventory: ~15,000 kg (liquid and vapour)
- Design temperature range: -40°C to +50°C

INCIDENT DESCRIPTION

During a maintenance activity, a flanged joint on the liquid ammonia line between
the high-pressure receiver and the expansion valves was disturbed. The joint had
been incorrectly re-assembled following a routine inspection, with two bolts not
tightened to the specified torque.

Three days later, during the early morning shift, a pinhole leak developed at the
flange. Ammonia vapour began escaping into the machine room. The toxic gas detector
in the machine room alarmed at 25 ppm (the STEL threshold).

The operator who responded to the alarm entered the machine room without wearing
the supplied-air breathing apparatus (SABA) that was stored in a locker adjacent
to the room. The operator was overcome by ammonia vapour within 30 seconds and
collapsed.

A second operator was alerted and activated the emergency shutdown (ESD) system,
which closed all automatic isolation valves and stopped the compressors. The
second operator then donned SABA equipment and retrieved the first operator.

The first operator was hospitalised with chemical burns to the respiratory tract
and eyes. He recovered fully after 4 days.

An estimated 200 kg of ammonia was released before the ESD isolated the system.
The vapour cloud dissipated outside the building through roof ventilation.

ROOT CAUSES

1. Maintenance failure — flange reassembled incorrectly after inspection
2. No torque verification procedure for flanged joints on ammonia lines
3. No leak test conducted after reassembly of the flanged joint
4. Operator did not wear PPE (SABA) when responding to gas alarm

IMMEDIATE CAUSES

- Incorrectly torqued flange bolts → gradual leak → escalating release
- Operator entered hazardous atmosphere without respiratory protection

CONTRIBUTING FACTORS

- Maintenance procedures did not specify torque values for flange reassembly
- PPE (SABA) was stored in a locker adjacent to the hazardous area rather than
  in a safe location outside the machine room
- The initial alarm response procedure did not clearly state that SABA must be
  worn before entering the machine room
- Inadequate supervision of maintenance work on ammonia lines

CHEMICALS INVOLVED

- Anhydrous ammonia (NH3): colourless toxic gas, IDLH 300 ppm, STEL 25 ppm,
  LEL 15%, UEL 28%, TLV-TWA 25 ppm, refrigerant grade > 99.99% purity
- Cooling water (secondary circuit)

CONSEQUENCES

- 1 operator hospitalised (respiratory and eye chemical burns, full recovery)
- 200 kg ammonia release — no community impact (building contained vapour)
- Production shutdown for 72 hours during repair and investigation
- Regulatory notification to environmental authority required

PROCESS PARAMETER DEVIATIONS

- Pressure: LESS (local pressure drop at leaking flange)
- Concentration: MORE (ammonia concentration in machine room atmosphere)
- Flow: AS WELL AS (unintended flow of ammonia vapour to machine room)
- Procedure: OTHER THAN (maintenance procedure not followed correctly)

SAFEGUARD PERFORMANCE

Safeguards that worked:
- Toxic gas detector in machine room (alarmed at 25 ppm — functioned correctly)
- Emergency shutdown (ESD) system (activated successfully by second operator)
- Roof ventilation system (dispersed vapour to atmosphere safely)
- Automatic isolation valves (closed on ESD — minimised total release quantity)

Safeguards that failed or were inadequate:
- Maintenance procedure (no torque specification, no post-maintenance leak test)
- PPE availability and use (SABA stored in hazardous area, operator did not use it)
- Alarm response procedure (did not mandate PPE use before entry)

RECOMMENDED SAFEGUARDS

1. Revise all maintenance procedures for ammonia lines to specify:
   - Required torque values for all flanged joint bolt patterns
   - Mandatory post-reassembly pressure test / leak detection before returning
     the line to service
   - Sign-off by a second qualified technician (independent verification)

2. Relocate all emergency PPE (SABA, chemical suits) to a clearly marked safe
   refuge location at least 15 metres from the machine room entry, upwind.

3. Revise the alarm response procedure to state clearly:
   "DO NOT ENTER machine room after gas alarm without SABA — don PPE at safe
   refuge point before entry."

4. Install continuous monitoring of flanged joint integrity on high-consequence
   ammonia lines (acoustic emission or fixed gas detectors at critical joints).

5. Implement a permit-to-work (PTW) system requiring sign-off from the production
   manager before any work begins on the ammonia refrigeration system.

6. Conduct HAZOP review of the maintenance and inspection process (as a process in
   itself) to identify further latent failure modes.

7. Install additional toxic gas detectors at all maintenance access points to the
   ammonia system.

LESSONS LEARNED

1. Maintenance activities on toxic gas systems carry high inherent risk.
   Every reassembly of a pressure joint must be treated as safety-critical and
   followed by a leak test before the system is returned to service.

2. Emergency PPE must be accessible WITHOUT entering the hazardous area.
   Storing SABA inside or immediately adjacent to the hazardous area defeats its
   purpose — operators will not be able to don it before exposure.

3. Alarm response procedures must be specific about PPE requirements and must be
   regularly drilled so that the correct response is automatic under stress.

4. The ammonia refrigeration industry has a well-documented history of maintenance-
   related releases. IIAR (International Institute of Ammonia Refrigeration)
   guidelines and ASHRAE 15 provide specific requirements to prevent such incidents.

Reference: Based on AIChE EPSC Loss of Containment data and IIAR Bulletin 110
guidance on ammonia refrigeration system safety.
